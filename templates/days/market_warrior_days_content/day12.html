📖 Part 1: What is Fundamental Analysis?

Definition

Fundamental Analysis is the method of evaluating a company's intrinsic (true) value by examining its business model, financial health, management quality, competitive position, and economic environment.

🔍 The Key Difference:

Technical Analysis Fundamental Analysis
Studies: Price charts, patterns, volume Studies: Financial statements, company performance
Goal: Predict price movement timing Goal: Determine if company is undervalued/overvalued
Timeframe: Short to medium term Timeframe: Medium to long term
Tools: Charts, indicators, patterns Tools: Financial ratios, earnings reports, industry data
Question: WHEN should I trade? Question: WHAT should I trade?

📖 Real Example:

Technical Trader sees: "Stock broke above $50 resistance with high volume - BUY signal!"

Fundamental Analyst sees: "Company losing money for 3 years, debt increasing, negative cash flow - AVOID regardless of chart!"

Smart Trader combines both: "Chart looks good BUT fundamentals are terrible - I'll skip this one and find a better opportunity!"

🔄 Part 2: Two Approaches to Fundamental Analysis

1. Top-Down Approach

Start Broad, Narrow Down

Step 1: Analyze the overall economy (Is it growing or in recession?)

Step 2: Choose strong industries within that economy (Tech booming? Healthcare growing?)

Step 3: Pick the best companies within that industry

Example:

  • Economy: Strong GDP growth, low unemployment ✓
  • Industry: Electric vehicles sector booming ✓
  • Company: Find best EV company with strong financials ✓

Best for: Finding new opportunities, riding macro trends

2. Bottom-Up Approach

Start Specific, Consider Context

Step 1: Find great companies first (strong financials, good management)

Step 2: Check if their industry is healthy

Step 3: Verify economy supports growth

Example:

  • Company: Found Company X with excellent profits and low debt ✓
  • Industry: Check if sector is growing or declining
  • Economy: Verify economic conditions are favorable

Best for: Stock pickers, finding hidden gems

✅ Which Approach to Use?

Beginners: Start with Top-Down (easier to follow trends)

Warren Buffett style: Bottom-Up (find great companies first)

Most professionals: Combine both approaches!

🏗️ Part 3: The Three Pillars of Fundamental Analysis

Every fundamental analysis examines three levels:

Pillar 1: Economic Analysis (Macro Level)

What you look at: Overall economy health and trends

Key factors exist:

  • GDP growth (economy expanding or contracting?)
  • Inflation rates (prices rising or stable?)
  • Interest rates (cost of borrowing money)
  • Employment data (people working or unemployed?)

Why it matters: Strong economy = Companies make more money = Stocks rise

Note: You'll learn detailed economic indicators in Day 28

Pillar 2: Industry Analysis (Sector Level)

What you look at: The specific sector/industry performance

Key questions:

  • Is this industry growing or declining?
  • How competitive is it? (Many competitors or few?)
  • Are there regulations affecting it?
  • What are the future trends? (Tech advancing? Demand increasing?)

Example: Renewable energy is booming (growing industry) vs coal is declining (dying industry)

Pillar 3: Company Analysis (Micro Level)

What you look at: Individual company's performance and value

Key elements:

  • Financial Statements: Income Statement, Balance Sheet, Cash Flow
  • Management Quality: Is leadership experienced and trustworthy?
  • Competitive Advantages: What makes this company special?
  • Financial Ratios: Numbers that reveal company health (tomorrow's lesson!)

Note: You'll dive deep into financial ratios and company analysis in Day 13

📄 Part 4: Financial Statements Overview

📌 Important Note

This is just an OVERVIEW today. Tomorrow (Day 13) you'll learn how to actually calculate and use financial ratios. Today = Understanding what exists, Tomorrow = Learning how to use it!

The Three Core Financial Statements

1. Income Statement (Profit & Loss)

What it shows: How much money company made or lost over a period

Key items: Revenue (sales), Expenses, Net Income (profit)

Think of it as: Your personal monthly income and expenses statement

2. Balance Sheet

What it shows: Company's assets, liabilities, and equity at a specific moment

Key items: What company owns (assets) vs what it owes (liabilities)

Think of it as: Your net worth calculation (what you own minus what you owe)

3. Cash Flow Statement

What it shows: Actual cash moving in and out of business

Key items: Operating cash, Investing cash, Financing cash

Think of it as: Your bank account statement showing money in and out

💡 Why All Three Matter

A company can show profit on Income Statement but have no cash (cash flow problem)!

A company can have lots of assets but huge debt (balance sheet problem)!

You need to look at ALL THREE to understand true financial health!

🔍 Part 5: Where to Find Fundamental Data

Free Resources (What Professional Traders Use)

🌐 Essential Free Platforms:

  • Yahoo Finance - Financial statements, stock data, news (most popular!)
  • Google Finance - Quick overview, charts, market news
  • SEC EDGAR - Official company filings directly from government (10-K, 10-Q reports)
  • Company Investor Relations - Go to company website → Investor Relations section
  • MarketWatch - Comprehensive analysis and economic calendar

Key Documents You'll Read

📄 10-K Report (Annual)

What it is: Comprehensive annual financial report filed with SEC

What's inside: Full year results, business description, risk factors, financial statements

When: Filed once per year

📄 10-Q Report (Quarterly)

What it is: Quarterly financial update

What's inside: 3-month results, management discussion, updated financials

When: Filed every 3 months (4 times per year)

📄 Earnings Calls

What it is: Company executives discuss results with analysts

What to listen for: Future guidance, management confidence, business strategy

When: After each quarterly earnings release

✅ Beginner's Action Plan:

  1. Start with Yahoo Finance (easiest interface)
  2. Look up any stock ticker (e.g., AAPL for Apple)
  3. Click on "Financials" tab to see statements
  4. Read the "Summary" section for quick overview
  5. Tomorrow you'll learn what all those numbers mean!

📊 Part 6: Introduction to Financial Ratios

⚠️ Just an Introduction Today

Financial ratios are CRITICAL for analysis, but we're just introducing them today. Tomorrow (Day 13) you'll learn the formulas, how to calculate them, and interactive exercises. Today = Know they exist, Tomorrow = Master them!

What Are Financial Ratios?

Financial ratios are mathematical calculations using numbers from financial statements that help you quickly assess company health, profitability, and value.

Think of them like vital signs: Just as doctors check blood pressure, temperature, heart rate - traders check P/E ratio, ROE, debt levels!

Main Categories (Overview Only)

📈 Valuation Ratios

Tell you if stock is cheap or expensive (e.g., P/E ratio)

You'll calculate these tomorrow!

💪 Profitability Ratios

Show how well company makes money (e.g., ROE, profit margins)

You'll master these tomorrow!

🛡️ Financial Health Ratios

Reveal if company can pay its debts (e.g., debt-to-equity, current ratio)

You'll practice these tomorrow!

📊 Efficiency Ratios

Measure how well company uses resources (e.g., asset turnover)
