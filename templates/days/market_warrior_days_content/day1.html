💡 What Is Trading?

📈 Definition

Trading means buying or selling financial instruments (like stocks, currencies, and crypto) to make a profit from price changes. If the price increases after you buy, you can sell it higher. If it goes down after you sell short, you can buy it back lower.

⏰ Time Horizons

Investing typically spans years/decades, focusing on long-term growth by holding assets.

Trading, however, can be short-term (minutes, hours, or days) or medium-term (weeks), aiming to profit from shorter-term price fluctuations.

🧠 Skills Required

Successful trading requires market knowledge, sharp analytical skills, emotional control, and unwavering discipline. It's more than just guessing; it's a learned skill that involves deep understanding of market dynamics and your own behaviour.

📈 3.1 Stocks

"Stocks let you own a tiny piece of a company" is the most fundamental concept of this market. When you buy a company's stock, you become a shareholder. While you won't be calling the shots in the boardroom with a few shares, you do get rights to the company's earnings and assets.

In-depth: The stock market is often considered a great starting point for new traders because it's tangible. You can research a company you know and understand, like Apple or Nike, and follow its progress in the news. The market is regulated by bodies like the SEC in the US and the FCA in the UK, which adds a layer of security.

Examples: Buying one share of Tesla means you own a small fraction of the electric car company. If the company performs well, the value of your shares typically increases. If they do poorly, the value can fall.

💱 3.2 Forex

Forex is the largest market by daily volume (trillions of dollars). This immense liquidity means you can buy or sell major currency pairs with very little difficulty, and the price is less susceptible to manipulation by a single large trader.

In-depth: Forex trading involves buying one currency while simultaneously selling another. Currencies are traded in pairs (e.g., EUR/USD). The value of a currency is constantly changing based on a country's economic stability, interest rates, and political news. For instance, if the US Federal Reserve increases interest rates, the US dollar might strengthen against other currencies, making it more valuable.

Examples: A trader might buy the Euro against the US Dollar (EUR/USD) if they believe the European economy is going to perform better than the US economy, leading to a stronger Euro relative to the Dollar.

₿ 3.3 Crypto

Crypto is relatively new and can swing wildly in price. This high volatility is both the main attraction and the biggest risk. Because crypto markets are decentralised, they operate 24/7, which means you can trade at any time.

In-depth: Cryptocurrencies are a new asset class based on blockchain technology. While Bitcoin and Ethereum are the most well-known, there are thousands of other digital assets. The decentralised nature of crypto means it is not controlled by any central authority, which appeals to many investors seeking freedom from traditional financial systems. However, the lack of traditional regulation in some jurisdictions also means there is less consumer protection compared to regulated markets.

Examples: The price of Bitcoin can move hundreds or even thousands of dollars in a single day, offering opportunities for significant gains or losses. Ethereum is another popular cryptocurrency with a wide range of use cases beyond just a store of value.

🥇 3.4 Commodities

Commodities (think gold rushes and oil booms) have been traded for centuries. Unlike stocks, commodities are tangible assets that are used in everyday life. Their value is driven by global supply and demand.

In-depth: Commodities are essential to the global economy. They can be a good way to diversify a portfolio because their value often moves independently of stocks. They are also often seen as a hedge against inflation. During times of high inflation, the price of assets like gold can increase as investors seek to protect their purchasing power.

Examples: Gold is a popular safe-haven asset, often sought after during economic uncertainty. Crude Oil is another major commodity, with its price being highly sensitive to global events and supply changes.

🤔 Interactive Element

Think about something you use every day, like your phone or your car. What commodities are in it? (e.g., lithium, copper, steel, crude oil). This helps connect real-world products to the commodities market.

📊 3.5 ETFs

ETFs simplify trading in all sectors (like tech or healthcare). An ETF, or Exchange-Traded Fund, is a basket of different securities that is traded on a stock exchange. This allows you to invest in a broad range of assets in a single trade.

In-depth: ETFs are great for beginners because they instantly diversify your portfolio, reducing risk. Instead of choosing a single stock, you can buy an ETF that tracks an entire industry or index. For example, an S&P 500 ETF holds shares in the 500 largest US companies.

Examples: If you believe the technology sector will grow, you could buy a technology sector ETF, which holds a collection of stocks from companies like Apple, Microsoft, and Google.

⚡ 3.6 CFDs

CFDs let you bet on price moves (up or down) but come with higher risk. A CFD, or "Contract for Difference," is a derivative product that allows you to speculate on the price movement of an asset without actually owning it.

In-depth: CFDs are leveraged products. This means you only need to put down a small percentage of the total trade value to control a much larger position. While this can amplify profits, it can also amplify losses, which is why they carry a higher risk. They are a popular tool for short-term traders.

Examples: If you believe the price of a stock will fall, you can open a short CFD position. If the price does fall, you'll profit. If it rises, you'll incur a loss.

Safety note on CFDs: Beginners should practice on a demo and avoid using leverage until they are fully trained.

  • • Stocks: Offer tangible ownership of companies, often seen as a foundational market for beginners due to their relative simplicity and direct correlation with company performance.
  • • Forex: Boasts exceptionally high liquidity and 24/5 availability—ideal for those who prefer flexible trading hours and direct exposure to global macroeconomic events.
  • • Cryptocurrencies: New, fast-moving, and 24/7—attractive for those seeking excitement, higher volatility, and participation in emerging financial technologies.
  • • Commodities: Tied to real-world supply and demand, offering alternative ways to diversify a portfolio and potentially hedge against inflation.
  • • ETFs: Provide instant diversification in a single trade, useful for broad market or sector exposure with lower individual stock selection effort.
  • • CFDs: Offer the ability to go long or short on almost any market with leverage, but come with significantly higher inherent risks due to amplified gains and losses.
