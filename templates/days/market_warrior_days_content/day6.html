🛡️ Part 1: Stop Loss - Your Safety Net

What is a Stop Loss?

A stop loss is an automatic order that closes your trade when the price reaches a predetermined level, limiting your maximum loss on any single trade.

Why Stop Losses Are NON-NEGOTIABLE:

  • Protects your capital: One bad trade won't wipe you out
  • Removes emotion: No panic selling or hoping it comes back
  • Enforces discipline: You accept the loss and move on
  • Keeps you in the game: Live to trade another day

Types of Stop Losses

Fixed Stop Loss

Set at a specific price level

Example: Buy at $150, stop at $145

Best for: Clear support/resistance levels

Percentage Stop Loss

Set as % below entry price

Example: Buy at $150, 5% stop = $142.50

Best for: Consistent risk management

Trailing Stop Loss

Moves up with price, locks in profits

Example: 5% trailing stop follows price up

Best for: Trending markets, capturing big moves

Volatility Stop Loss

Based on ATR (Average True Range)

Example: 2x ATR below entry

Best for: Adapting to market conditions

💡 Real Trading Example - Fixed Stop Loss

Scenario: Trading Apple Stock

  • Entry Price: $150.00
  • Stop Loss: $145.00 (3.3% risk)
  • Position Size: 100 shares
  • Maximum Loss: $500 ($5 × 100 shares)

What happens: If Apple drops to $145, your broker automatically closes the position. You lose $500, but you're protected from further losses if it drops to $130 or lower.

📖 Horror Story: Trading Without Stop Loss

John's Mistake: John bought Tesla at $900 without a stop loss, thinking "it always comes back." The stock dropped to $700. Instead of accepting a manageable $200 loss, he held on hoping. It eventually fell to $550—a $350 loss per share!

Lesson: A $200 planned loss beats a $350 catastrophic loss every time. ALWAYS use stop losses!

Where to Place Your Stop Loss

Stop Loss Placement Strategies:

1. Below Support (Long Positions):

Place stop 1-2% below nearest support level


2. Above Resistance (Short Positions):

Place stop 1-2% above nearest resistance level


3. Percentage-Based:

Risk 1-2% of account per trade


4. ATR-Based:

1.5-2x ATR away from entry (adapts to volatility)

💡 Stop Loss Placement Example

Setup: You identify a stock with strong support at $100

Entry: You buy at $105 (just above support)

Stop Loss Options:

  • Conservative: $99 (below support with buffer)
  • Moderate: $99.50 (tight below support)
  • Percentage: $102.50 (2.5% risk)

Why below support? If price breaks support, the trend is invalidated. No point staying in the trade!

🎯 Part 2: Take Profit - Locking In Gains

What is Take Profit?

A take profit order automatically closes your trade when your profit target is reached, ensuring you actually realize your gains.

Why Take Profit Matters:

  • Removes greed: Locks in profit before reversal
  • Enforces targets: Stick to your trading plan
  • No monitoring needed: Set and forget
  • Psychological relief: Profit is secured

Take Profit Strategies

Fixed Target

Set at specific price level

Example: Buy at $150, TP at $165

$15 profit per share

Risk-Reward Ratio

Based on your stop loss distance

Example: Risk $5, target $15 (1:3 ratio)

Professional standard!

Resistance-Based

Set just before resistance

Example: Resistance at $170, TP at $169

High probability of filling

Partial Profit

Scale out in stages

Example: Take 50% at +5%, 50% at +10%

Balances profit and potential

💡 Complete Trade Example with Stop Loss & Take Profit

Stock: Microsoft (MSFT)

  • Entry: $300.00
  • Stop Loss: $290.00 (3.3% risk, $10 loss)
  • Take Profit: $330.00 (10% gain, $30 profit)
  • Risk/Reward: 1:3 (Risk $10 to make $30)

Outcome: If you're right, you make $30. If you're wrong, you only lose $10. You only need to be right 33% of the time to profit!

The Risk-Reward Ratio

Understanding Risk-Reward

Formula: Risk/Reward = Distance to Stop Loss ÷ Distance to Take Profit


Professional Standards:

❌ 1:1 ratio = Not worth it

✅ 1:2 ratio = Acceptable minimum

✅ 1:3 ratio = Professional standard

✅✅ 1:4+ ratio = Excellent opportunity

📖 Success Story: The Power of Risk-Reward

Sarah's Strategy: Sarah only takes trades with 1:3 risk-reward ratio. Out of 10 trades:

  • 6 losers: -$100 each = -$600
  • 4 winners: +$300 each = +$1,200
  • Net profit: +$600 with only 40% win rate!

Lesson: Good risk-reward ratios mean you can be wrong more than you're right and still profit!

⚡ Part 3: Leverage - The Double-Edged Sword

What is Leverage?

Leverage allows you to control a large position with a small amount of capital. It's like trading with borrowed money from your broker.

How Leverage Works:

Without leverage: You need $10,000 to buy $10,000 worth of stock

With 10:1 leverage: You need only $1,000 to control $10,000 worth of stock

The catch: Both profits AND losses are multiplied by 10!

Leverage Your Capital Position Size 5% Gain 5% Loss
No Leverage (1:1) £1,000 £1,000 +£50 (5%) -£50 (5%)
5:1 Leverage £1,000 £5,000 +£250 (25%) -£250 (25%)
10:1 Leverage £1,000 £10,000 +£500 (50%) -£500 (50%)
20:1 Leverage £1,000 £20,000 +£1,000 (100%) -£1,000 (100% WIPED OUT)

⚠️ The Leverage Trap

With 20:1 leverage, a 5% move against you = 100% account loss. With 50:1 leverage, a 2% move wipes you out. This is why 90% of leveraged traders fail!

Safe Leverage Guidelines

Beginners (0-6 months)

Maximum: 2:1

Learn without getting destroyed

Focus on risk management first

Intermediate (6-12 months)

Maximum: 5:1

Once consistently profitable

Still room for mistakes

Advanced (1+ years)

Maximum: 10:1

Only if profitable for 12+ months

Strict risk management required

Never Recommended

20:1 or higher

Extreme risk of total loss

Even pros avoid this

📖 Cautionary Tale: The Leverage Disaster

Mike's Mistake: Mike had £5,000 and used 50:1 leverage to control a £250,000 position. The market moved 2.5% against him—a normal daily fluctuation. Result? His entire £5,000 account was wiped out in minutes.

What he should have done: Used 2:1 leverage for a £10,000 position. The same 2.5% move = £250 loss (manageable and recoverable).

💰 Part 4: Margin - Understanding Your Requirements

What is Margin?

Margin is the deposit (collateral) required to open and maintain a leveraged position. Think of it as a "good faith deposit" to your broker.

Margin Calculation:

Formula: Required Margin = Position Size ÷ Leverage


Example:

Position Size: £10,000

Leverage: 10:1

Required Margin: £10,000 ÷ 10 = £1,000

Types of Margin

Initial Margin

Minimum deposit to open position

Example: Need £1,000 to open £10,000 trade

Maintenance Margin

Minimum to keep position open

Usually: 50-75% of initial margin

Free Margin

Available for new trades

Formula: Equity - Used Margin

Margin Level

Account health indicator

Formula: (Equity ÷ Margin) × 100%

⚠️ The Margin Call - Every Trader's Nightmare

What is a Margin Call?

When your losses reduce your margin level below the broker's requirement (usually 100%), you get a margin call. The broker will:

  1. Warn you to deposit more funds, OR
  2. Automatically close your positions (at the worst time!)

💡 Margin Call Example

Scenario:

  • Account Balance: £10,000
  • Open Position: £50,000 (5:1 leverage)
  • Required Margin: £10,000
  • Margin Level: 100%

What happens if trade goes -10% against you:

  • Loss: £5,000 (10% of £50,000)
  • New Equity: £5,000
  • Margin Level: (£5,000 ÷ £10,000) × 100 = 50%
  • MARGIN CALL! Broker closes position immediately!

📖 How to Avoid Margin Calls

  1. Use less leverage - Never max out your margin
  2. Always use stop losses - Exit before margin call
  3. Keep margin level above 200% - Safety buffer
  4. Don't add to losing positions - Accept the loss
  5. Monitor positions regularly - Especially high leverage

📚 Extra Learning Notes

📏 The 1% Rule

Never risk more than 1-2% of your account on a single trade. If you have £10,000:

  • Max risk per trade: £100-200
  • Allows 50-100 losing trades before bust
  • Professional standard for longevity

🎯 Win Rate vs Risk-Reward

Key Insight: High win rate isn't everything!

  • 40% win rate + 1:3 R:R = Profitable
  • 60% win rate + 1:1 R:R = Break even
  • Focus on R:R, not just wins!

🔄 Trailing Stop Strategy

How to use trailing stops effectively:

  • Start with fixed stop loss
  • Once +10% profit, switch to trailing
  • Trail at 50% of current profit
  • Example: +£1000 profit = £500 trailing stop

💼 Position Sizing Formula

Calculate exact position size:

Position Size = (Account × Risk%) ÷ (Entry - Stop)

Example:

£10,000 account, 1% risk, Entry $150, Stop $145

= (£10,000 × 0.01) ÷ ($150-$145) = 20 shares
