🔍 Part 1: Where to Find Stock Research Data

Free Stock Research Platforms

🌐 Top Free Resources:

  • Yahoo Finance - Free financial statements, analyst ratings, historical data
  • Google Finance - Quick overview, price charts, news
  • SEC EDGAR - Official company filings (10-K annual reports, 10-Q quarterly)
  • MarketWatch - Comprehensive stock analysis and market news
  • Seeking Alpha - Community analysis and earnings transcripts
  • Company Investor Relations - Direct source from company websites

What Documents to Read

📄 10-K Annual Report

What it is: Comprehensive annual financial report filed with SEC

What to read:

  • Business description and risk factors
  • Financial statements (Income Statement, Balance Sheet, Cash Flow)
  • Management Discussion & Analysis (MD&A)

📄 10-Q Quarterly Report

What it is: Quarterly financial update (filed every 3 months)

What to read:

  • Recent quarter results vs previous quarter
  • Year-over-year growth trends
  • Any significant changes or events

📄 Earnings Call Transcripts

What it is: CEO/CFO discuss quarterly results with analysts

What to look for:

  • Future guidance and projections
  • Management confidence and tone
  • Answers to analyst questions

💰 Part 2: Key Financial Ratios (The Essential 8)

1. P/E Ratio (Price-to-Earnings)

P/E Ratio = Stock Price ÷ Earnings Per Share (EPS)

📖 Example:

Stock Price: $100

EPS: $5

P/E Ratio: 100 ÷ 5 = 20

Meaning: Investors pay $20 for every $1 of earnings

📊 How to Interpret P/E:

  • P/E < 15: Potentially undervalued (or slow growth)
  • P/E 15-25: Fair value for most stocks
  • P/E > 25: Expensive (or high growth expected)
  • Compare with industry average! Tech P/E = 30, Banks P/E = 12

2. EPS (Earnings Per Share)

EPS = Net Income ÷ Outstanding Shares

✅ What to Look For:

Growing EPS = Healthy Company!

Example: EPS growing from $2 → $3 → $4 over 3 years = 100% growth!

Always check EPS growth trend, not just one number!

3. P/B Ratio (Price-to-Book)

P/B Ratio = Market Price Per Share ÷ Book Value Per Share

📊 Interpretation:

  • P/B < 1: Stock trading below book value (potential bargain)
  • P/B 1-3: Normal range
  • P/B > 3: Stock valued higher than assets (usually tech/growth)

4. ROE (Return on Equity)

ROE = (Net Income ÷ Shareholder's Equity) × 100

🎯 ROE Benchmarks:

  • ROE > 15%: Excellent! Company uses equity efficiently
  • ROE 10-15%: Good
  • ROE < 10%: Poor - company struggles to generate profits
  • Warren Buffett targets ROE > 20%!

5. Debt-to-Equity Ratio

Debt-to-Equity = Total Debt ÷ Total Equity

⚠️ Risk Assessment:

  • Ratio < 0.5: Conservative (low debt, safe)
  • Ratio 0.5-1.5: Moderate (balanced)
  • Ratio > 2: High risk! Too much debt

2008 Crisis Lesson: Companies with high debt collapsed first!

6. Current Ratio (Liquidity)

Current Ratio = Current Assets ÷ Current Liabilities

💧 Liquidity Check:

  • Ratio > 2: Very liquid (can easily pay bills)
  • Ratio 1-2: Adequate liquidity
  • Ratio < 1: DANGER! May struggle to pay short-term debts

7. Dividend Yield

Dividend Yield = (Annual Dividend Per Share ÷ Stock Price) × 100

📖 Example:

Stock Price: $50

Annual Dividend: $2

Dividend Yield: (2 ÷ 50) × 100 = 4%

Meaning: You earn 4% annually just from dividends!

8. Operating Margin

Operating Margin = (Operating Income ÷ Revenue) × 100

📊 Profitability Measure:

  • > 20%: Excellent margins (very profitable)
  • 10-20%: Good
  • < 5%: Thin margins (competitive/tough industry)

📈 Part 3: Step-by-Step Stock Research Process

Step 1: Screen for Candidates

Start with a stock screener to filter thousands of stocks:

  • P/E ratio < Industry average
  • ROE > 15%
  • Debt-to-Equity < 1
  • Positive earnings growth last 3 years

Step 2: Check Financial Health

Review the company's financial statements:

  • Is revenue growing year-over-year?
  • Are profit margins improving or declining?
  • Is debt manageable?
  • Does company have positive cash flow?

Step 3: Evaluate Management

  • Read CEO letter to shareholders (10-K)
  • Check if management owns stock (aligned interests)
  • Review management's track record
  • Listen to earnings call for confidence and honesty

Step 4: Compare with Competitors

Never analyze in isolation! Compare key ratios:

  • Which company has best P/E in industry?
  • Which has highest ROE?
  • Which is gaining market share?
  • Which has strongest balance sheet?

Step 5: Final Decision

Combine all analysis into final verdict:

  • BUY: Strong fundamentals, undervalued, growing
  • HOLD: Fair value, wait for better price
  • AVOID: Overvalued, weak fundamentals, declining
