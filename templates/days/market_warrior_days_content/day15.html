🎲 Part 1: Understanding Risk Management

What is Risk Management?

Risk Management is the process of identifying, assessing, and controlling threats to your trading capital. It's not about avoiding risk (that's impossible in trading), but about managing risk so you can survive losing streaks and thrive in winning streaks.

🔑 Key Risk Management Principles:

  • Capital Preservation: Your #1 job is to NOT lose money
  • Consistent Position Sizing: Never risk more than 1-2% per trade
  • Risk-Reward Ratio: Only take trades with 1:2 or better (risk $1 to make $2+)
  • Stop Loss Discipline: ALWAYS use stop losses - no exceptions!
  • Diversification: Don't put all eggs in one basket

The Mathematics of Survival

Understanding these numbers will change your trading forever:

Loss % Gain Needed to Break Even Reality Check -10% +11% Easy to recover -20% +25% Challenging but doable -30% +43% Very difficult -50% +100% Nearly impossible! -75% +300% Game over

🚨 The Asymmetric Nature of Losses

If you lose 50% of your account, you need to make 100% just to break even! This is why capital preservation is MORE important than profits.

Example: $10,000 account loses 50% = $5,000. To get back to $10,000, you need to make 100% return on remaining $5,000. That could take YEARS!

📊 Part 2: The 1-2% Rule

Why Professional Traders Risk Only 1-2% Per Trade

The Math Behind the Rule

With 1% risk per trade:

  • You can lose 100 trades in a row before account reaches zero
  • That's statistically impossible even for worst traders!
  • You have psychological safety to stick to your plan
  • Each loss feels manageable, not devastating

The 1% Rule in Action

$10,000 Account Example:

  • 1% risk = $100 per trade
  • Entry: $150, Stop Loss: $145 = $5 risk per share
  • Position Size: $100 ÷ $5 = 20 shares
  • If stopped out: Lose exactly $100 (1% of account)
  • Can lose 100 times before account zeroes out

The 2% Rule for Aggressive Traders

$10,000 Account Example:

  • 2% risk = $200 per trade
  • Entry: $150, Stop Loss: $145 = $5 risk per share
  • Position Size: $200 ÷ $5 = 40 shares
  • If stopped out: Lose exactly $200 (2% of account)
  • Can lose 50 times before account zeroes out

⚠️ Never Risk More Than 2%

Even 2% is considered aggressive! Most professionals use 0.5-1% per trade. Why?

  • Losing Streaks Happen: Even with 60% win rate, you can lose 5-7 in a row
  • Psychology Matters: Small losses don't trigger emotional decisions
  • Compounding Works: Small wins on small risk add up faster than you think

🎯 Part 3: Position Sizing Formula

How to Calculate Exact Position Size

📐 The Position Size Formula

Position Size = (Account Size × Risk %) ÷ (Entry Price - Stop Loss)

💡 Complete Calculation Example

Setup:

  • Account Size: $20,000
  • Risk Per Trade: 1%
  • Entry Price: $85.00
  • Stop Loss: $83.00

Step-by-Step Calculation:

  1. Calculate Risk Amount: $20,000 × 1% = $200
  2. Calculate Risk Per Share: $85 - $83 = $2 per share
  3. Calculate Position Size: $200 ÷ $2 = 100 shares

Result: Buy 100 shares at $85

  • Total Investment: $8,500
  • If stopped out at $83: Lose $200 (exactly 1%)
  • Capital preserved for next trade

⚖️ Part 4: Risk-Reward Ratio

The Second Pillar of Risk Management

What is Risk-Reward Ratio?

Risk-Reward Ratio compares how much you're risking vs how much you can potentially make.

Formula: R:R = (Target - Entry) ÷ (Entry - Stop Loss)

Risk:Reward Win Rate Needed Verdict 1:1 50% ❌ Poor - no edge, break even at best 1:2 33% ✅ Good - minimum standard 1:3 25% ✅✅ Excellent - professional level 1:5 16% 🌟 Outstanding - master trader

💡 R:R Ratio Examples

Example 1: Good Trade (1:2)

  • Entry: $100
  • Stop Loss: $98 (Risk: $2)
  • Target: $104 (Reward: $4)
  • R:R = $4 ÷ $2 = 1:2 ✅

Example 2: Bad Trade (1:0.5)

  • Entry: $100
  • Stop Loss: $95 (Risk: $5)
  • Target: $102.50 (Reward: $2.50)
  • R:R = $2.50 ÷ $5 = 1:0.5 ❌
  • NEVER take this trade! Need 67% win rate just to break even!

The Golden R:R Rules

  1. Minimum 1:2: Never risk more than half of potential reward
  2. Target 1:3: Aim for triple your risk for highest probability
  3. Adjust Risk: If R:R is poor, wait for better entry or skip trade
  4. Scale Out: Take 50% profit at 1:2, let rest run to 1:3+

🔒 Part 5: Stop Loss Strategies

Types of Stop Losses

1️⃣ Fixed Dollar/Percentage Stop

Method: Risk fixed amount per trade (e.g., $100 or 2%)

Pros: Easy to calculate, consistent risk

Cons: Doesn't consider market structure

Best for: Beginners, consistent risk management

2️⃣ Technical Stop Loss

Method: Place stop below support (long) or above resistance (short)

Pros: Respects market structure, logical placement

Cons: May risk more than 2% if level is far

Best for: Experienced traders, swing trading

3️⃣ Volatility-Based Stop (ATR)

Method: Use Average True Range (ATR) × multiplier (e.g., 2×ATR)

Pros: Adapts to market volatility, avoids premature stops

Cons: More complex calculation

Best for: Advanced traders, volatile markets

4️⃣ Time-Based Stop

Method: Exit trade after X hours/days if target not hit

Pros: Prevents capital being stuck in dead trades

Cons: May exit before move happens

Best for: Day traders, scalpers

⛔ NEVER Do These:

  • Moving stop loss further away - This turns small loss into huge loss!
  • Removing stop loss - "I'll just wait for it to come back" = Account killer
  • Mental stops only - You WILL freeze when it matters. Always use hard stops!
  • Tight stops on volatile stocks - You'll get stopped out by noise
  • Wide stops to "give it room" - Violates 1-2% rule, destroys account
