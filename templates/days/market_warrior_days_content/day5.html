📊 Part 1: What is a Market Index?

The Market's Report Card

A market index is a measurement of a section of the stock market, calculated from the prices of selected stocks. It serves as a performance indicator for that particular market or sector.

Why Indexes Matter:

  • Benchmarking: Compare your portfolio performance
  • Market Sentiment: Gauge overall market health
  • Economic Indicator: Reflect economic conditions
  • Investment Vehicle: Can invest directly in indexes

🔍 How Indexes Are Calculated

Price-Weighted

Higher-priced stocks have more influence

Example: Dow Jones Industrial Average

If Apple at $150 rises 1%, it impacts the index more than a $30 stock rising 1%

Market-Cap Weighted

Larger companies have more influence

Example: S&P 500, FTSE 100

Apple's $3 trillion market cap influences the index more than a $10 billion company

Equal-Weighted

All stocks have the same influence

Example: Some ETFs use this method

Each company affects the index equally regardless of size

🌍 Part 2: Major Global Indexes

📍 United States

S&P 500 (^GSPC)

Contains: 500 largest US companies

Weighting: Market-cap weighted

Represents: ~80% of US stock market

Top holdings: Apple, Microsoft, Amazon, Nvidia

Dow Jones (^DJI)

Contains: 30 blue-chip companies

Weighting: Price-weighted

Represents: Major industry leaders

Historic significance: Oldest US market index (1896)

Nasdaq 100 (^NDX)

Contains: 100 largest non-financial companies

Weighting: Market-cap weighted

Represents: Technology and growth stocks

Character: More volatile, tech-heavy

🇬🇧 United Kingdom

FTSE 100

Contains: 100 largest UK companies

Companies: BP, Shell, HSBC, AstraZeneca

Note: Heavy in energy, finance, mining

FTSE 250

Contains: Next 250 largest UK companies

Character: More UK-focused than FTSE 100

Use: Better UK economy indicator

💡 Part 3: Using Indexes for Trading & Investing

📈 For Long-Term Investors

Index Fund Investing Strategy

Instead of picking individual stocks, invest in the entire index:

  • S&P 500 Index Fund (VOO, SPY): Own all 500 companies
  • Automatic diversification: Risk spread across 500 stocks
  • Historical returns: ~10% annually over 50+ years
  • Low cost: Expense ratios as low as 0.03%

Warren Buffett's advice: "A low-cost S&P 500 index fund is the best investment most people can make."

⚡ For Active Traders

Index Futures

Trade S&P 500 futures (ES), Dow futures (YM)

Advantage: 24-hour trading, leverage available

Use case: Hedging, speculation on market direction

Index CFDs

Trade index movements with no ownership

Advantage: Go long or short easily

Popular: US30 (Dow), US500 (S&P), GER30 (DAX)

Index ETFs

Trade like stocks (SPY, QQQ, DIA)

Advantage: High liquidity, no expiration

Strategy: Swing trading index movements

💡 Real Trading Example

Scenario: You believe the tech sector will rally

  1. Buy Nasdaq 100 ETF (QQQ) at $350
  2. Set stop loss at $340 (3% risk)
  3. Set take profit at $370 (6% gain)
  4. Position size: 10 shares = $3,500 investment
  5. Risk: $100 | Reward: $200 | Risk/Reward: 1:2 ✓

Result: One trade gives exposure to 100 companies instead of picking one stock!

📚 Part 4: Index Correlations & Market Relationships

Understanding Market Connections

Key Relationships:

  • S&P 500 ↑ → Individual stocks ↑: Rising tide lifts most boats
  • VIX (Fear Index) ↑ → Indexes ↓: Inverse relationship
  • Dollar Index ↑ → Commodities ↓: Negative correlation
  • Tech stocks ↑ → Nasdaq ↑: Sector influence

⚠️ Common Index Trading Mistakes

1. Ignoring correlation: Trading S&P 500 and Dow together = double exposure

2. Chasing news: Index reactions are often already priced in

3. Over-leveraging: Index futures have high leverage—use carefully

4. Forgetting dividends: Some index funds pay, others don't

📊 Extra Learning Notes

📈 Index Rebalancing

Indexes periodically add/remove companies:

  • S&P 500 rebalances quarterly
  • Stocks added often surge (buying pressure)
  • Stocks removed often drop
  • Traders watch for rebalancing announcements

🕐 Index Trading Hours

Stock indexes: During market hours

Index futures: 23 hours/day

Index CFDs: Varies by broker

Tip: Futures provide after-hours sentiment

💰 Cost Comparison

Index fund: Own 500 = Low cost (0.03%)

Active fund: Manager picks = High cost (1%+)

Winner: Index funds for most investors
