💡 Part 1: Core Trading Terms

🔍 1.1 Bid, Ask, and Spread

📊 Bid Price

The highest price a buyer is currently willing to pay for an asset.

Think of it: The best offer someone is making to buy from you right now.

📈 Ask Price (Offer)

The lowest price a seller is currently willing to accept.

Think of it: The cheapest price at which you can buy right now.

💰 The Spread

The difference between bid and ask prices. This is your immediate trading cost.

Formula: Spread = Ask - Bid

Tighter spread = Lower cost

💡 Real Example: Apple Stock (AAPL)

Current market shows:

  • Bid: $174.95 (buyers willing to pay)
  • Ask: $175.05 (sellers willing to accept)
  • Spread: $0.10 per share

Cost Impact: Buying 100 shares instantly costs you $10 in spread alone (100 × $0.10)

🎯 1.2 Long vs Short Positions

📈 Going LONG

Meaning: Buying an asset expecting price to RISE

Profit when: Price goes UP ⬆️

Maximum loss: 100% (if price goes to zero)

Maximum profit: Unlimited

📉 Going SHORT

Meaning: Selling borrowed assets expecting price to FALL

Profit when: Price goes DOWN ⬇️

Maximum loss: UNLIMITED (price can rise infinitely)

Maximum profit: 100% (if price goes to zero)

⚠️ Critical Warning on Short Selling

Short selling has UNLIMITED risk because prices can theoretically rise forever. Many beginners have been wiped out by unexpected price spikes. Always use stop losses when shorting!

⚡ 1.3 Leverage and Margin

What is Leverage?

Leverage is using borrowed money to control a larger position than your capital allows. It's a double-edged sword that amplifies both profits AND losses.

📊 Leverage Example

Your capital: £1,000

Leverage: 10:1

Position size: £10,000

  • If market moves +5%: You gain £500 (50% on your £1,000)
  • If market moves -5%: You lose £500 (50% of your £1,000)
  • If market moves -10%: You're WIPED OUT completely

💵 Margin

The deposit required to open a leveraged position.

Example: 10:1 leverage = 10% margin requirement

To control £10,000, you need £1,000 margin

🚨 Margin Call

When losses deplete your margin below minimum requirements.

Result: Broker demands more funds or closes your positions

Prevention: Never over-leverage!

🎯 Order Types in Action

📱 Scenario: Trading Tesla (TSLA)

Current price: $200.00

Your strategy using different orders:

  1. Market Order: "Buy NOW!" → Executes at $200.10 (ask price)
  2. Limit Buy: Set at $198 → Waits for price to drop
  3. Stop Loss: Set at $190 → Protects against >5% loss
  4. Take Profit: Set at $220 → Secures 10% gain
  5. Trailing Stop: 5% below peak → Follows price up, locks gains

🤔 Think About This

If you buy at $200 with a stop loss at $190 and take profit at $220:

Risk: $10 per share (5%)

Reward: $20 per share (10%)

Risk/Reward Ratio: 1:2 (This is good!)

🔍 Part 3: Market Mechanics

📊 3.1 Liquidity, Volume, and Volatility

💧 Liquidity

How easily an asset can be traded without moving its price.

High Liquidity: Apple, EUR/USD

Low Liquidity: Penny stocks, exotic pairs

Impact: Higher liquidity = tighter spreads

📊 Volume

Total number of shares/contracts traded in a period.

High volume confirms: Breakouts, trend changes

Low volume suggests: Weak moves, potential reversals

Rule: Trade with volume, not against it

⚡ Volatility

How much and how fast prices move.

High volatility: Crypto, meme stocks

Low volatility: Bonds, utilities

Remember: Volatility = Opportunity + Risk

💱 3.2 Pips, Points, and Ticks

Understanding Price Movements

  • Pip (Forex): Usually 4th decimal place (0.0001)
  • Point (Stocks): Usually $1 or £1 move
  • Tick (Futures): Minimum price movement allowed

💱 Pip Calculation Example

EUR/USD moves from 1.1850 to 1.1860

Movement = 10 pips (0.0010)

If trading 1 standard lot (100,000 units):

Profit/Loss = 10 pips × $10 per pip = $100

📚 Extra Learning Notes

🧠 Psychology of Spreads

The spread is your first enemy in every trade. You start at a loss equal to the spread the moment you enter. This is why:

  • Scalpers need tight spreads
  • Day traders avoid wide-spread assets
  • Long-term investors care less about spreads

🎯 The 2% Rule

Professional traders never risk more than 2% of their account on a single trade. If you have £10,000:

  • Maximum risk per trade: £200
  • If stop loss is 50 pips away
  • Position size = £200 ÷ 50 = £4 per pip

⚠️ Leverage Reality Check

Statistics show 90% of leveraged traders lose money. Why? They use too much leverage. Start with:

  • Maximum 2:1 as beginner
  • 5:1 with 6+ months experience
  • 10:1 only when consistently profitable
