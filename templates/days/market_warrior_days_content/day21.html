💸 Part 1: Understanding Dividends

What Are Dividends?

Definition: Cash payments companies make to shareholders from their profits.

Think of it like: Owning a rental property that pays you monthly rent, except it's a business paying you quarterly.


Example: You own 100 shares of Apple at $150 each ($15,000 investment)

  • Apple pays $0.96/share annually in dividends
  • You receive: 100 × $0.96 = $96/year
  • That's free money just for holding the stock!

Key Dividend Metrics

📊 1. Dividend Yield

Formula: (Annual Dividend ÷ Stock Price) × 100

Example: Stock pays $2/year, costs $50 = 4% yield

Good yield: 2-6% (higher might be risky)

💰 2. Dividend Payout Ratio

Formula: (Dividends Paid ÷ Net Income) × 100

What it means: % of profits paid as dividends

Sweet spot: 30-50% (sustainable)

Red flag: >80% (might cut dividend)

📈 3. Dividend Growth Rate

What it shows: Annual increase in dividend payments

Example: Microsoft increased dividend 10%/year for 10 years

Why it matters: Growing dividends = inflation protection

Types of Dividend Stocks

Type Yield Growth Risk Examples

🏦 Part 2: Understanding Bonds

📚 What Are Bonds?

Simple Definition: You loan money to companies/governments, they pay you interest.

Think of it like: You become the bank, earning interest on your loan.

Bond Components

Face Value

Amount paid at maturity

Usually: $1,000

Coupon Rate

Annual interest rate

Example: 5%/year

Maturity Date

When you get money back

1-30 years

Credit Rating

Safety of bond

AAA (best) to D

💡 Real Bond Example

You buy: $10,000 US Treasury Bond, 5% coupon, 10-year maturity

You receive:

  • $500/year for 10 years (5% of $10,000)
  • Your $10,000 back after 10 years
  • Total received: $15,000 ($5,000 interest + $10,000 principal)

Risk: Almost zero with government bonds!

Types of Bonds

Bond Type Issuer Yield Risk Best For
Treasury Government 3-5% Zero Safety
Corporate Companies 4-8% Medium Income
Municipal Cities/States 2-4% Low Tax-free income
High Yield Risky companies 8-15% High Risk-takers

Dividend Reinvestment (DRIP)

🔄 The Power of Reinvesting

What is DRIP? Automatically using dividends to buy more shares


$10,000 investment example:

  • Without DRIP: $400/year income × 20 years = $8,000
  • With DRIP: Same investment grows to $26,533!
  • That's $16,533 in profit vs $8,000 - double the return!

Pro tip: Most platforms like eToro and Trading 212 offer automatic dividend reinvestment - turn it ON!

⚠️ Part 4: Risks & Red Flags

🚩 Dividend Traps to Avoid

  • Yields above 8%: Often unsustainable, company might be in trouble
  • Declining revenue: Can't pay dividends if not making money
  • Payout ratio >100%: Paying more than they earn = dividend cut coming
  • No dividend history: Avoid companies without 5+ years of payments

📉 Bond Risks

  • Interest rate risk: Bond prices fall when rates rise
  • Default risk: Company might not pay back (check credit ratings!)
  • Inflation risk: 3% bond loses money if inflation is 5%
  • Liquidity risk: Hard to sell before maturity

📚 Extra Learning: Advanced Strategies

🎯 Dividend Capture Strategy

Buy stock just before ex-dividend date, sell after receiving dividend

Example: Stock trades at $100, pays $2 dividend tomorrow

Reality: Stock usually drops by dividend amount - not free money!

🏗️ Bond Laddering

Buy bonds maturing in different years for steady income

Example: Buy 5 bonds maturing in 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 years

Benefit: Money available every year + protection from rate changes
